# Room 04 — supplied story

Story supplied by the user. This author reference preserves the narrative used for the interactive adaptation; it is not displayed as a walkthrough in the game.

## 1. An address with something missing

The road ends a few steps before the house.

You check the card again.

**ROOM 04.**

There is no street name. Below the number, someone has drawn a small arrow, but it points towards the edge of the paper.

The house stands in grass so green it looks freshly remembered. Four windows. A pale roof. A washing line with one cloud caught on it.

Your suitcase has several pale rectangles where bright stickers used to be. You carry that side against your leg.

When you reach the porch, there is already a blue chair beside the door. A square of sunlight lies neatly on its seat, although the porch is in the shade.

You put down your suitcase.

Somewhere inside, a bell rings once.

You wait for someone to answer it.

Eventually, you sit.

## 2. Things that travel slowly

The first flowerpot arrives while you are checking the windows.

It has one yellow flower and a saucer slightly too large for it. It settles beside your shoe.

Another arrives sideways. A third keeps stopping to shake a little gravel out of its saucer, then hurrying to catch up.

None of them seems particularly interested in your card.

They do, however, know how to open the door.

Inside, the rooms are arranged by weather. There is a room with the soft light before rain, a room where sheets dry in five minutes, and a narrow room containing the last warm part of a day.

The pots disagree about which is best.

You begin travelling together.

One holds a door with its saucer. Another leans across a gap so you can catch the loose end of a curtain. When you fall asleep on the floor, something nudges the blue chair close enough for you to rest your head.

Sometimes a pot follows a patch of sunlight through a different doorway.

Sometimes you meet it again.

Once, a little white pot travels beside you for an entire staircase, then rolls away at the landing. Much later, you find a single white petal tucked into the fold of your map.

## 3. The west-facing window

You arrange the chairs facing east.

The pots gather at the west-facing window.

You turn the house.

They shuffle across the floor to the other side.

You turn it again.

A teaspoon falls off the table.

On the third turn, you pull the front step out of the grass and put it on the roof.

For a moment, everything is exactly where you have decided it should be.

Then it starts raining inside.

Only inside.

Beyond the windows, the afternoon remains clear.

Two saucers tap against the doorway. After a while, you hear them moving down the path.

The yellow flower stays in the garden, facing west.

You stand beside the kettle, holding a towel that is getting wetter.

When the rain finally stops, there is a clean circle on the windowsill where a pot used to sit.

## 4. Water on the second floor

The next staircase leads into a shallow swimming pool.

Sunlight moves across the tiles. The water is warm around your ankles. Through a low window, you can see the underside of a cloud.

You have definitely taken a wrong turn.

At the far edge of the pool is the blue chair.

A flowerpot you have never met is sitting beside it. When you approach, it pushes a folded towel towards you.

You take off your wet shoes.

The pot waits.

Somewhere, a kettle clicks off. You cannot see a kitchen.

Later, you find another place to sit in a corridor that opens into a meadow. And another on a balcony with no building beneath it.

The furniture is different each time.

Once, a chair has a short leg. Someone has already folded a piece of paper beneath it.

You sit down carefully.

It holds.

## 5. A small alteration to the entrance

When you find the first house again, its number has changed.

The step is still on the roof.

It takes most of the afternoon to bring it down.

You make a little ramp. The first version is too steep. The second catches the edge of a saucer. You sit in the grass and sand it until a leaf can slide down without snagging.

Then you move the chair away from the doorway, where someone could rest without having to come inside.

The tall pot arrives near sunset.

It tries the ramp, examines the room, and goes directly to the west-facing window.

You carry the water over there.

Another pot pauses at the edge of the garden. You set a shallow dish beside the path.

It drinks.

Then it continues on its way.

You rinse the dish and put it back.

The clean circle remains on the windowsill. Sunlight crosses it for a few minutes each afternoon.

## 6. Room 04

One day, the bell rings and all four windows open at once.

Beyond each is a different path.

There are no more rooms upstairs.

You carry the table into the grass.

The pots arrange themselves around it with the same small disagreements as before. One prefers the ground. One needs a book beneath its saucer. The yellow flower turns its whole pot towards the evening light.

You bring out cups.

Then a few more.

When you lift the blue chair, you notice a paper label underneath the seat.

**ROOM 04.**

The corners of the label have been worn soft.

You look at the card in your pocket.

Then you put the chair beside the table.

Your suitcase fits beneath it.

From somewhere along the path comes the small scrape of a saucer.

You move the kettle closer.

There is still enough water.
